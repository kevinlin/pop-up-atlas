# Trolls films: sound and voice design notes

Research for an original sound and voice pass on Pop-up Atlas: Troll Edition. Compiled 2026-11-21.

Covers Trolls (2016), Trolls World Tour (2020), Trolls Band Together (2023), and the TrollsTopia series (2020-22) where it helps. The goal is design principles. Nothing here is meant to be copied; see "Do not copy" below.

Tags: [S] sourced (see Sources), [F] fan wiki, retail page or search snippet (lower confidence), [I] my inference, not sourced.

The richest source is the Tonebenders podcast ep. 136 with the World Tour sound team [S1]. No transcript was published, so I transcribed the public audio locally (faster-whisper small.en). Timestamps like `[05:40]` refer to that audio. Wording is paraphrased and may carry transcription errors.

## Top findings

1. **Foley plays the real material, then softens it with fabric.** The team listened to the 2016 film's Foley stems on their own and found its rule: a surface sounds like the real thing it stands for, even when it looks like felt on screen [S1 04:32]. The rule had exceptions. A denim invitation got paper Foley with a cloth layer mixed in, because purely real sound "didn't feel right" [S1 05:40-06:14]. For us: paper and card pops with a felt layer. Felt alone is nearly silent.
2. **Mouth sounds carry the comedy.** "The directors love vocal sounds", which make things "funnier and less scary". Bubble pops on landing and a creature's wings were the Foley artist's own voice, and one director voiced a gag sound himself [S1 26:44-27:53]. For us: build the fold pop from recorded lip and cheek pops. That fits the films' method and is our own material.
3. **Effects play as percussion in the band.** The team had each song's tempo before its key. They nudged bumps "to be in a musical place", like "a member of the orchestra" [S1 14:16-15:22]. Foley went to higher hits when the music was busy in the low end [S1 11:28]. In a high-energy song the directors pushed back on added rumbles, rips and creature vocals [S1 07:49-08:18]. For us: keep pops on the 312.5 ms grid and keep footsteps and pops above the chime register.
4. **The stories turn on singing, colour and harmony.** A grey troll lost his colour and stopped singing; his song brings everyone's colour back [S4]. World Tour ends with trolls building a rhythm from body sounds, then singing together, with "different voices" in harmony [S5]. Band Together hinges on siblings singing in perfect sync [S6]. For us: walking already plays a tune. Give each tile type its own voice in the same key, and let simultaneous folds form chords.
5. **Character contrast is the voice brief.** The lead is "insanely optimistic, but not too naive" against a "negative" partner [S2]. The glitter troll's voice is auto-tuned [S9]. The antagonists are "mean, petulant and cranky" [S3], and their world was designed as polyester and plastic [S7]. For us: one bright, breathy tiny-troll voice, a flatter "grumpy" variant for bumps, and pitch-snapped sparkle on anything glittery.

## 1. How trolls sound

- Lead: "insanely optimistic, but not too naive" (co-director, 2016) [S2]. Lead actor on the role: "more heart and mischief and sass" [S11].
- Pairing: "this super positive character and this negative character", built for "contentious chemistry" [S2]. The grey troll doesn't sing, dance or hug [S3].
- Trolls as a group: "a constant dance in their step and a song on their lips"; they "live to sing, dance and hug" [S3].
- Glitter troll: auto-tuned voice [S9].
- Several side voices came from the co-director's scratch track: "sometimes the voices just stick" [S2]. Rough, playful vocals were good enough to ship.
- One character on the antagonists' side was directed to "imagine a sleeping angel on your tongue" [S2]. Soft and breathy can work on the "villain" side too.
- Antagonists: "mean, petulant and cranky" [S3]; designed as 1970s "suburbanites", "polyester, plastic" [S7]. World Tour's rock antagonist needed sounds that were "somewhat scary" but still fun, since "technically a family film" [S1 21:35-22:22].
- Crowds: World Tour crowds were animated like a spectrum analyser, following the music [S12, S13]. The finale crowd makes rhythm with body sounds (heartbeat, then beatbox), then sings [S5].
- 2016 library cues on the fan wiki include "Baby Vocals Playful" and "TOY - ANIMAL TOY: SINGLE SQUEAK 03" [F, S14]. Baby giggles and toy squeaks as sweeteners are on-genre.
- [I] Troll speech sits high, fast, with sing-song rising contours; the grumpy voice is lower, flatter, clipped; the antagonists are low and heavy. No source states this as a design rule.

## 2. Sound effects: team and approach

### Teams

- Trolls (2016): Erik Aadahl and Ethan Van der Ryn, supervising sound editors / sound designers; Foley by Dan O'Connell and John T. Cucci [F, S15].
- World Tour (2020): Paul Hackner, supervising sound editor and SFX re-recording mixer; Jessie Pariseau, supervising Foley editor and SFX editor; Scott Millan, dialogue and music re-recording mixer [S1].
- Band Together (2023): Hackner names it alongside World Tour as his proudest work [S10]. His supervising credit is from an IMDb search snippet [F].
- Not found: an interview with the 2016 team, or any Band Together sound interview. Soundworks Collection, Mix, A Sound Effect and Post had no Trolls sound piece I could find. The 2016 Post feature URL now returns 404.

### Approach (World Tour team, [S1])

- Three tests for every sound: does it feature the music; does it tell the story; is the surface more exciting played real or played as fabric [06:53-08:29].
- Blend real and fabric most of the time. Go fully real when the story needs a punch, e.g. a cloth rip as a creature bursts through a quilt [06:16].
- Water stayed water. A silky sound for the satin-look river was tempting and a tinsel waterfall was tried, but "it needs to feel like water" [08:29-09:04].
- Glitter changes a creature's sound: a glitter-coated creature was redone with "crinkly things" so it sounded like hardened glitter [25:27-25:43].
- Psychedelic moments got retro synths (rented Roland synths, early-80s and anime-style) instead of the genre's own instruments [20:13-20:42].
- Wings that had to cut through a loud rock song only worked as processed human vocals [26:13-26:33].

### Visual facts that set the palette

- Look built on "felts, velvet, macramé, and flocked materials"; glitter "is a big part of their world" [S3]. "Our grass was felt" [S7].
- Hair is a tool: trampolines, zip lines [S7].
- Scrapbooks narrate the lead's thoughts; the pages were real craft work, scanned and given depth [S7].
- Glitter animated partly on ones and partly on twos for a stop-motion feel [S12].
- A watch "reminds them to hug every hour" [S3]. The lead owns a cowbell, which another troll uses to lure the rest out of hiding [S4].
- 2016 fan-listed library cues: suction pops, "BIG BUBBLE POP, FANTASY", "Bull Whip Crack", "Single Small Bell", a cartoon "WHISTLE ZING" [F, S14].

Asked about, not sourced: how the hair whips/lassos, glitter bursts, cupcakes, critters or hug watch were sound-designed. The whip crack and small bell exist only as fan-listed library cues.

## 4. Per-moment treatment

Contract today (`docs/contracts.md` Audio): fold-up = noise pop + low thump + bell chime; fold-down = quieter pop; at most 8 voices. Per-tile timbres or vocal layers change that contract; update contract, code, tests and `docs/log.md` together.

### Original tiny-troll voice (design brief)

> Original voice for a thumb-sized felt troll. Gender-neutral, young-sounding but not a baby. Bright, slightly breathy, soft fuzzy edges. High pitch, as if a warm adult voice were raised 5-7 semitones with small-creature formants. Quick and bouncy, with sing-song rises at phrase ends; giggles easily. Mostly non-verbal: "hup", "ooh", "hee-hee", "wheee", "hmph", "eep", "ta-da". No accent cues, no catchphrases, and it must not resemble any known actor or existing animated character. Dry studio recording, no reverb.

- Grumpy variant (bumps only): same voice, 3-4 semitones lower, flat contour, clipped.
- Residents: smaller and squeakier than the troll, slightly muffled as if inside a felt house.
- Pitched vocals (hum, "ta-da") land on D or A so they sit in the key.
- [I] Per finding 2, a human performer doing Foley-style mouth sounds will probably beat AI voices for the pops and giggles. Record once, then pitch-shift.
- Every vocal counts toward the 8-voice limit; drop the voice before the chime.

## Do not copy

- Character, place and group names from the films and series, e.g. Poppy, Branch, Bergens, Chef, Bridget, Guy Diamond, Barb, Hickory, BroZone, Velvet and Veneer, Trollstice, Troll Village, Pop Village, Lonesome Flats.
- Phrases: "hug time" in any form, "Perfect Family Harmony", and any line of dialogue.
- Any song, lyric, melody or hook from the soundtracks, including the covers they use. Our tune comes only from the pentatonic walk generator.
- Film props as signature sounds: an hourly hug watch or bracelet that chimes, the lead's cowbell used as a summons, the zipper sound on the rock antagonist's bus.
- Any cast member's voice or delivery; no auto-tune pastiche of the glitter character.
- Audio from the films, trailers or toys, and the film-specific library cues listed on the fan wiki.

## Could not source

- Filmmaker or sound-team statements on specific vocal mannerisms (giggles, gasps, squeals, scatting, sing-song speech). Section 1's last bullet is my inference.
- Sound design of hair whips/lassos, glitter bursts, cupcakes, critters, the hug watch, the cowbell, or the pop-up cards.
- Anything from the 2016 sound team or the Band Together sound team in their own words.
- The TV series' sound; only TrollsTopia's premise is sourced.
