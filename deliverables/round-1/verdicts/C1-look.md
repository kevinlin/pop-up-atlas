## Overall

**Reliable blind picks: 6 of 6.** Each pair had a construction or material cue strong enough to make the call.

**Would the render wow a viewer? No.** The rainbow bridge and bright hair are recognizable at a glance, but the repeated planting and missing book edges make most shots read as a game map.

| Rank | Highest-impact fix | Expected visual effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Establish the physical book. Show the gutter and layered cover in wide shots, and expose at least one folded brace, glued tab, or lifted paper edge in close shots. | The viewer can see what the forest is made from and how it stands up. |
| 2 | Compose each view around one action. Group the scenery, vary its height by about 3:1, and give the traveller a clear silhouette at the bridge, on the path, or in the clearing. | The six views become distinct scenes. The eye finds the subject before it notices repeated assets. |
| 3 | Put craft detail where it changes an outline or casts a shadow. Prioritize loose hair fibers, scarf braids, petal rims, satchel seams, and wrapped bridge posts. | Wool, felt, yarn, and paper become distinguishable even where they share a color. |
| 4 | Relight from a broad warm side key with a weaker pink-violet fill. Keep contact dark at folds and feet, and concentrate glitter highlights on the stream and selected edges. | Layers gain separation, the traveller gains weight, and glitter appears to catch sunlight. |
| 5 | Set focus by subject distance for each shot. Hold the eyes and the object being used in focus, then increase blur smoothly toward nearer and farther objects. | The close-ups acquire miniature scale while the face, footing, and paper mechanism stay readable. |

**Lessons learned**  
One visible folded brace explains the pop-up premise better than a row of decorative cutouts.  
At this scale, a stray fiber, a loop of yarn, and the shadow under a lifted foot carry much of the photographic effect.
