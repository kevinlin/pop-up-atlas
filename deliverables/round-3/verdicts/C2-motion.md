### 1. Fold motion and spring feel · 6/10

The fold is legible. In row three of the opening sheet, the pink flower goes from flat to edge-on to face-up. In the walk burst, it folds flat again behind the troll. The mid-fold still shows why it works: the base stays planted while the flower sweeps around the crease. The rebound is much harder to see. Nine rises and nine closures register in the JSON, but few command attention like that flower.

1. Stage the first fold against bare paper. Leave one flower-head width clear around its sweep so the player can follow the tip and see the fixed crease.
2. Give the tallest flap about **0.5 s** to reach upright, up from 0.40 s. Keep the existing roughly **14° overshoot** and show its return against the same clear background.
3. Delay the next flap on that tile to **0.28–0.32 s** after the first, up from 0.18 s. Let the player recognize one hinge before a second shape starts moving.

### 2. Walk feel · 6/10

The offline sheet has a lively rise and a clear split stride. Beside the mushroom in gameplay, the boots repeatedly overlap and the torso stays nearly upright. I can see the beat. It takes more effort to tell which boot is carrying the troll.

1. Turn the chest and hips **15–20° toward the camera** on this route. Keep the feet tracking the path and check that both boots have distinct outlines at heel strike.
2. Lift the swing toe from half a boot-height to **about one boot-height**. Let the heel lead into contact so the foot describes an arc.
3. Keep the **2 steps/s** and the planted quarter-second. Put the lowest hip pose **60–80 ms after contact**, over the loaded boot. That gives the existing bob a cause.

### 3. Dance · 4/10

The offline back-facing jump is the most appealing pose in either character sheet. In the 4 fps gameplay strip, the face and red bag stay visible in every occupied panel. I can’t identify the turn or the held bow there. **Three seconds is a good length** for this gag.

1. Match the gameplay strip to the actual dance start and end. Check that the dance reaches full animation weight during the **0.75 s turn**. At 4 fps, at least one sample should clearly show the troll’s back and hide the bag.
2. Carry the side step **half a torso-width** across the page. Hold the raised mitten clear of the head for **0.2 s** so the opening gesture reads at game size.
3. Hinge the torso forward **about 35°** over planted feet for the existing **0.35 s bow**. In the offline finish, the lifted boot draws the eye and the pose reads as a kick.

### 4. Hair secondary motion · 5/10

The plume is easy to spot even when the boots disappear. Across the walk burst, its outer curve stays much the same as the head rises and falls. The sheets give little sense of the felt tip arriving late or recoiling when the troll stops.

1. Keep the root firm and let the upper third of the plume trail a change of head direction by **80–120 ms**.
2. On the deepest foot plant, aim for tip travel of **one-quarter head-width** relative to the scalp. Keep the clumps together so that bend reads as felt.
3. Give the stop one visible forward flick and a smaller return, settling in **0.35–0.45 s**. Judge it with the head already still.

### Overall

**Would a player be wowed? No.** The pink flower earns a second look, but the troll can walk past a mushroom and perform most of a dance without a comparably clear change of pose.

1. Give the first flower a clear silhouette through its full fold and rebound.
2. Make the back-facing dance pose reliably visible in gameplay.
3. Separate the walking boots and show the hips taking weight on each contact.

**Lesson learned:** At this camera distance, a fixed crease, a planted boot, and a momentarily hidden red bag each tell the player exactly what moved.
