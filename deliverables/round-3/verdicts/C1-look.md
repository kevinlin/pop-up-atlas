### Overall

**Reliable blind picks: 6 of 6.** **Would the render wow a viewer? No.** The rainbow bridge comes closest, but the smooth hair, exposed cream ground, and repeated plant shapes give away the render in every shot.

| Rank | Change | Expected visual effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build the book and its folding mechanism into each composition. Show a gutter or page edge, a glued foot, and at least one open V-fold. | A viewer can tell at a glance what supports the scenery and how it would close. |
| 2 | Restage around the traveller. Give the face and feet a clear silhouette, then flank the route with two distinct, large landmarks at different depths. | The eye finds the character first and can follow a route through the forest. |
| 3 | Rework the character materials, starting with swept, fraying hair. Give the scarf visible yarn twists and the felt body a soft fiber rim. | The most recognizable object holds up in close-up and establishes the scale of the whole set. |
| 4 | Use a broad warm side light and a weaker pink-violet fill. Anchor feet, tabs, and overlapping sheets with short contact shadows. Reserve peak highlights for a few glitter flakes. | Layers separate, objects sit on the page, and the glitter appears to catch sunlight. |
| 5 | Tune depth of field around the action in each shot. Keep the traveller and one construction detail sharp, then let blur build with distance. | The camera suggests miniature scale while preserving enough of the book to explain the scene. |

**Lessons learned**  
A visible paper hinge sells the pop-up premise even in a sparse shot.  
The quickest close-up checks are the edge of the hair and the shadow under the planted foot.
