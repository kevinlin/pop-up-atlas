### 1. Fold motion and spring feel · 6/10

I can see the pink mushroom rise and rebound in the 8 fps strip. Its underside flashes into view, which sells the spring. The sharp flower still shows the paper support, but during play it is harder to see where the page bends. The fold-down is easy to miss entirely.

1. Trigger the lead fold about 2 units ahead instead of 3, and the closing fold about 3 units behind instead of 4.5. That puts both actions roughly one tile closer together around the troll.
2. Turn the largest flap so its free edge sweeps sideways across a patch of bare page. Keep the crease visible from flat through upright. A player should be able to point to the hinge.
3. Give that flap 0.4–0.45 s to reach upright. Keep the roughly 15° overshoot and delay the first neighboring flap to about 180 ms so the rebound has room to read.

### 2. Walk feel · 6/10

The walk sheet has a clear reach and passing pose. In gameplay the boots still alternate, but the satchel hides part of the stride and the torso rides nearly level. The moving page conveys more speed than the puppet’s push-off.

1. Raise the body bob from 0.06 to about 0.09 units. Put the low point just after contact and the high point over the supporting boot.
2. Keep the 0.25 s plant. Roll off the toe and lift the swinging boot until there is half a boot-height of daylight beneath it in the game view.
3. Shift the hips over the planted foot and counter-swing the near arm past the satchel. That gives each step a visible change of weight above the boots.

### 3. Dance · 4/10

The airborne turn is the most appealing pose on either character sheet. In the 4 fps gameplay strip I see a small rise beside the blue paper flap, then much the same front-facing silhouette. Three seconds is a good length. The signature move gets lost inside it.

1. Give the turn 0.75 s with distinct front, profile, back and finishing poses. Check that the back pose actually appears in the gameplay capture.
2. Open with a sideways step of half a body-width and throw one arm above the head. This makes the dance recognizable even with the feet partly hidden.
3. Land low, then hold a crooked one-foot bow for 0.35 s before returning to idle. Give the player a finish they can catch.

### 4. Hair secondary motion · 5/10

The swept plume makes the troll easy to find. Across the walking burst its outline stays close to the same backward rake. The largest apparent shape change comes during the offline dance turn, where rotation alone accounts for some of it.

1. Let the head start each turn 80–120 ms before the tips follow. Judge the lag at the outer edge of the plume.
2. Shape the six locks into two or three visible clumps. Let one tip group trail its neighbor by 40–60 ms so the outline opens and closes.
3. On a stop, give the tips one 10–15° swing past rest and settle them within about 0.4 s. Keep the roots firm so the felt appears to bend.

### Overall

**Would a player be wowed? No.** The mushroom rebound catches the eye, but the page hinge, the planted step and the dance turn are all too easy to miss at play size.

1. Stage one unmistakable fold beside the troll, with the crease and moving edge visible together.
2. Make every planted step compress and lift the body.
3. Give the dance a turn and a held landing that survive the gameplay framing.

**Lesson learned:** The mushroom’s flashing underside communicates more motion than its measured overshoot. Give the boot, the turn and the hair tip equally clear visual cues.
